# Chinese brand example — 岫页

> Fictional teaching case. The native-language gate is part of the deliverable, not a disclaimer added afterward.

## Request

“给独立出版品牌‘岫页’做标志。读音是 `xiù yè`，想表达山中藏书的一页。可以融合山形、书页和中文，但不要做成古风印章。”

## Included visual artifact

The included symbol uses one page contour as two restrained mountain shoulders; the central opening is both a page seam and a sheltered valley. It does not alter, replace, or fuse the strokes of `岫页`.

The rendered artifact was inspected for the included monochrome, reverse, lockup, and responsive-size presentations. Geometry receives a `provisional pass`; native-language, target-market cultural, font-license, current prior-art, and trademark reviews remain pending.

## Good response shape

- Mode: `design`
- Given by the user: spelling, pronunciation, and intended meaning
- Unknown: target-region associations, preferred character style, and whether a native typographic reviewer is available

| Meaning | Visual structure | Language structure |
|---|---|---|
| Mountain recess | rising sides, sheltered inner gap | `岫` and its mountain radical |
| Page | rectangular plane, turned corner, open edge | `页` |
| Quiet discovery | protected void, measured rhythm | supplied brand story |

Recommended first route: use a page’s outer contour to form two restrained mountain shoulders and let the page opening become the sheltered gap. Keep `岫页` intact in the wordmark during concept selection. This creates one symbol without forcing three literal objects or damaging character anatomy.

An optional glyph-construction route may be explored only as **provisional** work. It must preserve stroke order cues, proportions, counters, and reading order closely enough for the intended audience. Do not invent etymology, homophones, or regional meaning from visual resemblance.

## Native-review gate

Before approving any altered Chinese glyph, obtain a native typographic review for the target market and record:

- whether both characters are recognized without explanation;
- likely misreadings caused by removed or shared strokes;
- unintended meanings, dialect associations, or cultural signals;
- whether the symbol and wordmark maintain the intended reading order;
- whether the chosen typeface and any modified glyphs are licensed for the delivery.

Status before that review: `provisional — symbol exploration may continue; altered-glyph approval is blocked`.

Visual QA and native-language review are different gates. Passing reduction tests cannot prove linguistic correctness, and native approval is not trademark clearance.
